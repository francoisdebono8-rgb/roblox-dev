# VISUAL CONTINUITY BLOCKER — LATEST CAPTURE AUDIT

**Status: ACTIVE / BLOCKING**

This audit is based on the latest ~32.8 s user-recorded Studio capture received after the visible-rupture recadrage. It is not a general design note. These are concrete visual defects that remain visible at normal speed and must be fixed before adding another large gameplay system.

The current problem is not only repetition. The body still exposes system boundaries. The player can see locomotion, attack, launcher, pursuit, reaction, landing and camera subsystems taking control in succession instead of one continuous physical performance.

Core rule:

> **No action may begin as if the previous action never happened.**

Every transition must inherit enough of the previous body's velocity, facing, support, pose, momentum and intent to read as one continuous fight.

Do not mark this pass complete because tests are green. It is complete only when the listed seams are no longer visible in real-time Studio playback.

---

## 1. CAMERA GEOMETRY FAILURE — HARD BLOCKER

### Latest capture: ~32.4–32.8 s

The camera penetrates / crosses arena wall geometry until the brick wall fills almost the whole screen. This is a severe presentation bug.

Required fix:
- camera must never enter solid arena geometry;
- wall/obstacle collision must push the camera inward or shorten the boom smoothly;
- no one-frame camera teleport from fight view to inside-wall view;
- recover smoothly when geometry clears;
- test specifically during knockback, wall splat, large VFX, wall-adjacent pursuit and camera shake;
- test corners as well as flat walls;
- camera collision must be evaluated after combat framing/shake offsets, not before an offset can push it back through geometry.

Acceptance:
- 3 x 60 s wall-heavy fights with zero camera penetration at 1x speed;
- frame-step review around every wall hit;
- neither fighter may be hidden by the camera being inside geometry.

---

## 2. LAUNCHER / TAKEOFF HAS VISIBLE STATE CUTS

### Latest capture: ~2.5–3.6 s

The sequence reads as several states rather than one physical action:
- grounded contact;
- defender abruptly becomes airborne;
- attacker reorganizes into an air-follow pose;
- both bodies become artificially arranged at combat distance;
- descent/landing changes state too abruptly.

The vertical motion is not fully explained by legs, pelvis and impulse. The viewer sees the launcher system.

### Latest capture: ~20.7–21.1 s

A second vertical transition near scenery again reads as an abrupt move from grounded exchange into airborne state. The launch/follow transition does not carry enough visible preparation and body momentum.

### Latest capture: ~30.6–31.6 s

Wall/air sequence: defender is raised/held near the wall while attacker remains below, then the scene transitions back through impact/recovery in discrete chunks. The defender's orientation and vertical state feel authored by the state machine rather than generated by the preceding hit.

Required fix:
- explicit anticipation/compression before strong vertical impulse;
- pelvis/root vertical velocity must match the animation's takeoff beat;
- feet must visibly leave support at the same moment the root begins the launch;
- attacker follow must derive from real velocity/intent, not instant pursuit placement;
- defender launch orientation must derive from contact direction and impulse;
- preserve angular momentum instead of resetting to a generic airborne pose;
- landing must include contact, compression and recovery rather than `air -> ground pose`;
- no hidden position correction that moves either actor by an obviously visible amount between adjacent frames.

Acceptance:
- launcher viewed at 1x must read without knowing the state names;
- at 0.5x, no visible positional snap > what the animated body can physically explain;
- takeoff, apex, descent and landing must each be visually connected.

---

## 3. POSE OWNERSHIP / ANIMATION SNAP

Visible in several exchanges, especially around:
- ~0.8–1.5 s;
- ~2.6–3.3 s;
- ~14.9–15.5 s;
- ~28.7–29.4 s.

Symptoms:
- torso/arms jump from locomotion or strike pose into another authored pose without enough blend;
- body facing changes faster than hips/feet can explain;
- an attack sometimes appears to start from a neutralized body instead of the pose reached by the preceding action;
- hit/recovery pose can replace the current body rather than inherit it.

Do not solve this with one global longer tween; that can make everything mushy. Fix ownership and inheritance.

Required technical audit:
- instrument transitions between locomotion / attack / pursuit / airborne / reaction / wall / landing owners;
- for each ownership handoff record previous root velocity, root facing, pelvis orientation, support foot, local joint pose and target pose;
- identify which system is hard-resetting or over-authoring joints/root orientation;
- preserve the outgoing pose as the true entry pose for the next action;
- use per-joint blend priorities where needed instead of replacing the full body at once;
- legs/support should not be overwritten by an upper-body action unless the move genuinely requires it.

Acceptance:
- no visible full-body snap during the listed windows;
- frame-step must show a continuous arc for head, pelvis, hands and feet through the transition.

---

## 4. FACING / TARGET TRACKING STILL LOOKS MAGNETIC

During close exchanges and air follow-ups the attacker often remains too perfectly arranged relative to the defender. This makes the system look like it is continuously correcting the actors rather than letting the player/body own the geometry.

Symptoms in the latest capture:
- rapid facing correction during close-range exchanges;
- airborne attacker/defender spacing reads too constant;
- pursuit can look like re-centering rather than acceleration and interception;
- side changes sometimes rotate the body before the feet/pelvis have earned the turn.

Required fix:
- reduce or cap auto-facing angular velocity by state;
- preserve whiffs and overshoot;
- pursuit should choose velocity/acceleration, not continuously solve actor position;
- contact spacing may prevent penetration but must not visually lock two bodies to a fixed distance;
- allow temporary non-facing states when momentum logically carries a fighter past the opponent;
- feet/pelvis initiate large grounded turns; torso may lead only within plausible limits.

Acceptance:
- two fighters can cross, overshoot, side-swap and miss without obvious magnetic correction;
- no repeated frame sequence where relative distance is visibly held constant while both actors move.

---

## 5. CONTACT / REACTION DOES NOT ALWAYS MATCH THE HIT

Across the capture, several impacts are readable primarily because of VFX, dust or displacement, while the two bodies do not always communicate the same contact at the same instant.

Required fix:
- body contact/reaction first, VFX second;
- reaction direction must match actual contact normal / attack vector;
- heavy displacement needs a visible body load before the target accelerates away;
- no target movement before the strike visually reaches contact unless the move is explicitly ranged/force-based;
- impact frame, sound, hitstop, camera impulse and defender reaction must share one authoritative contact timestamp;
- tune hitstop so it adds weight without freezing an incoherent pose.

Acceptance:
- disable large VFX in QA and every tested hit must still read clearly from body motion alone.

---

## 6. LANDING / GROUND RECOVERY STILL EXPOSES STATES

Seen after airborne exchanges around ~3.4–3.8 s and other knockdowns.

Symptoms:
- descent can end in a ground state too quickly;
- body sometimes appears to switch to knockdown/recovery instead of physically settling into it;
- feet/knees do not always absorb the fall before locomotion resumes;
- root speed and pose can disagree at landing.

Required fix:
- one continuous descent-to-contact path;
- landing pose derived from vertical/horizontal velocity and body orientation;
- visible compression or controlled roll where appropriate;
- preserve horizontal momentum through landing unless the move intentionally plants;
- recovery locomotion begins from the actual landed pose, not neutral stance.

---

## 7. ARENA PROPS ARE CAUSING VISUAL OCCLUSION

Latest capture around ~15.6–16.3 s and ~18.7–19.5 s: the central tree/post can dominate the foreground and partially hide the exchange.

The reactive-world system is useful. The current placement is not.

Required fix:
- remove large opaque test props from primary camera/combat lanes;
- keep destructible geometry mainly peripheral or placed with camera sightlines in mind;
- if a large object can enter the foreground, use camera-aware transparency/fade or framing avoidance;
- arena must remain a combat space first, reaction testbed second;
- no tree/post should hide the important contact moment in a normal 1v1 camera angle.

Do not add more scenery until the current sightline problem is solved.

---

## 8. LONG CHARGE / CLOSE BODY OVERLAP AROUND ~28.2–29.4 s

This sequence reads poorly:
- fighters remain very close while the action builds;
- silhouettes overlap and intent is hard to parse;
- then separation/impulse occurs abruptly;
- the visual cause/effect is weaker than the resulting displacement.

Required fix:
- clearer pre-impact spacing and silhouette separation;
- attacker body should show where the force is being generated;
- defender should not visually occupy the same body volume during charge/windup;
- if a bind/hold exists, author an explicit believable hold/contact relationship;
- resulting impulse must begin on the exact release/contact beat.

---

## 9. CONTINUITY CONTRACT — APPLY TO EVERY COMBAT TRANSITION

For every important state handoff, validate this vector:

`previous pose + root velocity + angular velocity + facing + support + opponent geometry + active intent -> next action entry`

Audit at minimum:
- run -> strike;
- strafe -> strike;
- strike -> strike;
- strike -> launcher;
- launcher -> pursuit;
- pursuit -> air strike;
- air strike -> fall;
- fall -> landing;
- knockback -> wall contact;
- wall contact -> rebound/splat/recovery;
- dodge -> strike;
- teleport/substitution -> reorientation;
- technique charge -> free movement;
- technique movement -> miss/world impact/player impact.

Hard rule:

> **No subsystem is allowed to silently reset the body because its own animation looks correct in isolation.**

The fight is the unit of quality, not the clip.

---

## 10. FIX ORDER

Do this before another major Embodiment subsystem:

1. camera geometry penetration;
2. launcher/takeoff/air/landing continuity;
3. pose ownership and joint/root handoff;
4. magnetic facing/pursuit corrections;
5. contact/reaction timestamp coherence;
6. ground recovery continuity;
7. arena occlusion and prop placement;
8. only then return to the larger visible-rupture gameplay goal.

Do not spend this pass adding moves, fighters, VFX catalogs, perception features or new architecture unless directly required to remove one of these visible defects.

---

# REQUIRED QA DELIVERABLE

Before declaring this blocker fixed:

- record a new 60–90 s Studio fight at normal speed;
- include at least 3 launchers, 3 landings, 2 wall interactions, 2 side-swaps/whiffs, 1 high-speed chase and sustained close combat;
- inspect the same recording at 0.5x and frame-step the transitions;
- report every remaining visible seam honestly;
- do not hide defects with camera cuts, VFX, motion blur or scenery;
- keep camera sightline clear enough to judge both full bodies.

Acceptance question:

> **Can a viewer watch the body continuously from one intention to the next without ever seeing the game switch systems?**

If not, continue.
